1Your legal papers

There are four documents. Three of them matter more than the will, because they work while you are still alive.

The four documents

DocumentWhat it doesHow to sign it in Maine
Will Says who gets your things after you die. Signed by you, plus 2 witnesses.
Financial power of attorney Lets someone you trust pay bills and handle money if you cannot. Most important document on this list. Notary required. Witnesses are not required in Maine.
Advance directive
(health care power of attorney / living will)		 Names who speaks to doctors for you, and says what treatment you do and do not want. 2 witnesses. No notary needed.
Transfer-on-death deed Passes the house straight to your children when you die, skipping probate court. Signed, notarized, and recorded at the Oxford County Registry of Deeds.
Read this before you use a transfer-on-death deed

A transfer-on-death deed avoids probate court. It does not, by itself, protect the house from MaineCare estate recovery. If MaineCare paid for your care, the State can still make a claim. Ask a lawyer which tool actually fits your goal — see Section 3.

Good news: Maine estate tax is almost certainly not your problem

Maine only taxes estates worth more than $7,160,000. A home and normal retirement savings are nowhere near that. You can stop worrying about this one.

Do this, in this order

  • Call Legal Services for Maine Elders at 1-800-750-5353. Free if you are 60+. Ask for help with a will and powers of attorney.
  • Pick your people. Who handles money? Who talks to doctors? They can be the same person or different people.
  • Do the financial power of attorney first. If a stroke or a fall happens without it, the family has to go to court to get guardianship. That is slow and expensive.
  • Do the advance directive next. Give a copy to your doctor and to Stephens Memorial Hospital in Norway.
  • Then the will.
  • Tell your family where the originals are kept. A document nobody can find does nothing.
Where to get these done, and roughly what it costs

Free: Legal Services for Maine Elders, 1-800-750-5353, for Mainers 60 and older. Also Pine Tree Legal Assistance, 207-774-8211.

Paid: A private elder law attorney. Worth the money if you own the house and are thinking about MaineCare, because the house is the part that goes wrong most often. Expect a flat fee for a basic set of documents.

Notary: Most banks and the Paris town office notarize. Call ahead.

Recording a deed: Oxford County Registry of Deeds, in South Paris.

2Getting help at home

Staying in your own home is almost always cheaper than moving, and Maine will pay for help that lets you stay. Most people never find out these programs exist.

Programs that send help to your house

ProgramWhat it pays forWho qualifies
MaineCare Section 19
Home & Community Benefits		 Personal care, homemaking, and nursing at home — the same level of care a nursing home gives. You need nursing-home level help, but want to stay home. Income and asset limits apply.
Section 63
In-Home & Community Support		 Help with bathing, dressing, meals, housekeeping. Lower level of need than Section 19. Some versions have a sliding fee instead of strict limits.
Meals on Wheels Hot or frozen meals delivered. Also a daily check on you. Through SeniorsPlus. No strict income test; donations requested.
HEAP
heating assistance		 Help paying for oil, propane, or wood. Income-based. Applying also gets you screened for free weatherization.

Who to call

SeniorsPlus 207-795-4010 Meals on Wheels, care coordination, Medicare counseling, Money Minders bill-paying help, Friendly Caller. Toll free: 1-800-427-1241. Community Concepts — heating help (HEAP) 207-743-7716 South Paris office. Applying for heating help also screens you for free weatherization and other aid.
Two benefits with NO asset test at all

These two only look at your monthly income — your savings and your house do not count. That makes them the easiest wins on this whole page.

  • Medicare Savings Program (QMB). Pays your Medicare Part B premium and your deductibles and copays. Income line: $2,461/month for one person, $3,337/month for a couple.
  • Low Cost Drugs for the Elderly (DEL). Pays up to 80% of what you still owe for prescriptions after Part D. Age 62+. Same income line.

If you are already on MaineCare or the Medicare Buy-In, you may be enrolled in DEL automatically.

How to apply for the home care programs

Easiest route: call the Aging & Disability Resource Center at 1-877-353-3771 or SeniorsPlus at 207-795-4010 and say you want a home care assessment. They walk you through it.

What happens next: a nurse or case manager visits and scores how much help you need with daily tasks — bathing, dressing, eating, moving around. That score decides which program fits.

Be honest in the assessment. People instinctively downplay how much they struggle. Describe your worst days, not your best ones, or you will be scored for less help than you actually need.

Bring: photo ID, Social Security and Medicare cards, proof of income (Social Security award letter, pension statements), recent bank statements, and a list of medications.

3Money, MaineCare, and the house

This is the section people find confusing and frightening. Here it is plainly.

Nursing home care in Maine costs roughly $11,000 a month. Almost nobody pays that out of pocket for long. MaineCare (Maine’s Medicaid) picks it up — but only once your countable savings are nearly gone.

The 2026 limits

SituationCountable assets allowed
One person applying, not married$10,000
Married, both applying, sharing a room$15,000 total
Married, one applying — the healthy spouse at home may keepup to $162,660
Monthly income the healthy spouse at home may keep$2,643.75
rising to $2,705 on July 1, 2026
If only one spouse needs care, the other is protected

This is the single most reassuring rule in the system. The healthy spouse staying at home keeps the house, keeps a car, keeps up to $162,660 in savings, and keeps a monthly income floor. Nobody is turned out of their home so the other can get care. Money moved between spouses is never penalized.

Two rules that surprise families

1. The 5-year look-back

When you apply, MaineCare examines the last 60 months of your finances. Any money or property you gave away for less than it was worth creates a penalty period — months when MaineCare will not pay, even though your money is already gone.

The math: amount given away ÷ about $11,000. Give away $110,000 and you get roughly 10 months with no coverage and no money.

So: do not give away money or sign the house over to the kids before talking to an elder law attorney. Good intentions here cause real harm. Timing is everything.

2. Estate recovery — what really happens to the house

While you are alive and living in it, your home does not count against the $10,000 limit. You will not be forced to sell it. That part is true and people are right to be relieved.

But: if you were 55 or older when MaineCare paid for your care, the State can file a claim against your estate after you die, to get back what it spent. In practice that often means the house is sold and the proceeds go to the State instead of to your children.

This is the fact families learn too late. It is not a reason to panic — it is the reason to plan early, while you still have options.

Legitimate ways to reduce countable assets

These are lawful and normal. They work because you are spending your own money on yourself, not giving it away — so no penalty applies.

  • Fix up the house. New roof, furnace, wiring, well pump. Also ramps, walk-in shower, grab bars, stair lift — which help you stay home longer anyway. The house is exempt, so money spent on it stops being countable.
  • Prepay the funeral and burial through an irrevocable arrangement with a funeral home. A real expense your family would otherwise face.
  • Pay off debts — mortgage, credit cards, the car loan.
  • Buy a reliable vehicle. One car is exempt.
  • Buy things you actually need — hearing aids, dental work, glasses, a good bed, a newer appliance.
Two exceptions worth asking a lawyer about by name

The caregiver child exception. If an adult child lived in the home for at least 2 years providing care that kept the parent out of a nursing home, the home may be transferred to that child with no penalty. This is a real and powerful rule. It requires documentation — so if a child is doing this now, start writing it down today: dates, tasks, and a letter from the doctor.

The sibling exception. A sibling with an equity interest who lived in the home for at least a year may also qualify.

There is also a 5-year irrevocable trust, which can protect the house — but only if it is set up and funded more than five years before you need care. It is not a do-it-yourself project. It needs an attorney and it needs time you may not have later. That is the argument for calling now rather than at the hospital.

Maine taxes on retirement income

  • Maine does not tax Social Security.
  • Maine allows a pension deduction of up to $49,824 per person — but it is reduced dollar-for-dollar by the Social Security you receive. Most retirees with a decent Social Security check get little or none of this deduction. This is the detail people get wrong when they estimate their tax bill.
  • Withdrawals from an IRA or 401(k) are taxable in Maine, at rates up to 7.15%.

4Where to live, from cheapest to most care

Nobody moves straight from home to a nursing home. There are rungs in between, and most families skip them without realizing.

OptionRoughly what it costsWhen it fits
1. Stay home as you are Taxes, heat, upkeep Now. Spend money on grab bars, better lighting, and a stair rail before you need them.
2. Stay home with paid help $0–$35/hour; often free through MaineCare Section 19 or 63 You need help bathing, dressing, cooking, or with medications. Almost always the right next step.
3. Residential care / assisted living $4,000–$8,000/month; some beds take MaineCare Home is no longer safe or manageable, but you do not need skilled nursing.
4. Nursing facility About $11,000/month; MaineCare pays once you qualify You need round-the-clock skilled care.
5. Maine Veterans’ Home, South Paris VA support plus MaineCare; often the best value for a veteran See Section 5. It is in your own town.
There is a veterans’ nursing home right here in South Paris

Maine Veterans’ Home — South Paris, 477 High Street. To get in you need only one or more days of active duty, an honorable discharge, and Maine residency. No combat required. No wartime service required. A spouse is eligible too, and may be admitted if she needs nursing or assisted-living level care.

Maine Veterans’ Home — South Paris 207-743-6300 477 High St, South Paris. Ask for the admissions coordinator. Ask to be put on the waiting list before you need a bed.

Nearby residential care and assisted living

These are the licensed options closest to home, so family can visit easily.

  • South Paris: Market Square Health Care Center · Paris Home · Elm Hill (69 Elm Hill Rd)
  • Norway: Norway Center · Spring Street Home (9 Spring St) · Beal Street (7 Beal St)
Call each one and ask these three questions
  1. “Do you accept MaineCare?” This varies by facility and changes over time. It is the question that decides everything, and no website is reliable on it.
  2. “If we pay privately first, do we have to leave when the money runs out?” Some places keep you and switch you to MaineCare. Some do not. Get the answer before you move in.
  3. “How long is the wait?” Then get on the list early. A waiting list costs nothing and you can always say no.

Before signing anything, call the Long-Term Care Ombudsman at 1-800-499-0229. They are free, independent, and will tell you a facility’s complaint history.

5Veteran benefits

Three years of active duty with no combat still qualifies for real benefits. Combat is not a requirement for any of the programs below.

First: find the DD-214

The discharge paper is called a DD-214. Every benefit on this page depends on it, and the exact service dates printed on it decide two of them. Find it and make three copies.

Lost it? Request a free replacement from the National Archives at archives.gov/veterans, or ask the Maine Bureau of Veterans’ Services to pull it for you — they do this routinely and it is free.

What is available, and what each one requires

BenefitWorthWhat it requires
Maine Veterans’ Home
South Paris		 Nursing and assisted living care in your own town 1+ day of active duty, honorable discharge, Maine residency. No wartime. No combat. Spouse also eligible. ✔ He qualifies.
VA health care
Togus, Augusta		 Primary care, specialists, prescriptions, hearing aids Generally 24 months of active duty or the full period called. Income-based enrollment groups. Worth applying.
VA Pension with Aid & Attendance Often $1,500–$2,800 a month toward care costs 90+ days active duty with at least one day during a wartime period, plus a care need and financial limits. Check the dates below.
Maine veteran property tax exemption $6,000 off the home’s assessed value Age 62+ and wartime service. Same date test. Apply at the Paris town office.
“He never saw combat” does not disqualify him

This is the most common misunderstanding about veteran benefits. The VA does not ask whether you were in combat. For VA Pension it asks only whether one single day of your service fell inside an official wartime window — anywhere in the world, in any job, including stateside desk duty.

Use the checker in Section 7 with the dates from the DD-214 to see whether his service falls inside one.

Money facts for VA Pension (2026)

  • Net worth limit: $163,699. Notably, the house you live in and up to 2 acres do not count, and neither does one vehicle or your household goods. This limit is far more generous than MaineCare’s $10,000.
  • Medical expenses reduce your countable income. Unreimbursed costs — insurance premiums, prescriptions, in-home aides, facility fees — come off your income. This is how most people actually qualify, even with a decent pension and Social Security.
  • The VA look-back is 36 months, not MaineCare’s 60. The two programs have different rules, and a move that helps with one can hurt the other. Another reason to get advice before shifting any money.
Maine Veterans’ Home — South Paris 207-743-6300 477 High St. Ask about the waiting list now, even if care is years away. VA Health Benefits & enrollment 1-877-222-8387 VA health care enrollment line. Togus VA Medical Center is in Augusta; the nearest outpatient clinic is in Rumford.
Use a free accredited representative, never a paid “benefits consultant”

Maine Bureau of Veterans’ Services and veterans’ organizations (VFW, American Legion, DAV) file VA claims for free and are accredited to do it. It is illegal to charge a fee for preparing an initial VA claim. If someone asks for a percentage of the benefit, walk away.

6Lower the property tax and household bills

These are small, certain, and quick. No lawyer needed. Several are simply forms nobody told you about.

ProgramWorthHow to get it
Homestead exemption $25,000 off assessed value One-time form at the Paris town office. From April 1, 2026 the full $25,000 applies to homeowners 65+ regardless of the town’s assessment ratio. Check you already have this — many people do not.
Property Tax Fairness Credit Up to $2,000, paid to you Claimed on the Maine income tax return. It is refundable, so you get a check even if you owe no tax and would not otherwise file. File a return anyway to claim it.
Veteran exemption $6,000 off assessed value Age 62+ with wartime service. One-time application with a copy of the DD-214.
State Property Tax Deferral The State pays your tax bill now For income-eligible owners 65+. Read the caution below. Filing deadline is April 1.
About the deferral program

It is a loan, not a gift. The State pays your property taxes and is repaid from your estate after you die, with interest. That is genuinely useful if cash is tight month to month. But it reduces what your children inherit, and it stacks with MaineCare estate recovery from Section 3. Decide it as part of the whole picture, not on its own.

Town of Paris — Assessor 207-743-2501 ext. 4 33 Market Square, Paris. assessor@parismaine.org — Ask: “What exemptions are currently on my property, and what am I missing?” Also ask for the current assessed value of your property.
Other bills worth attacking
  • Heating: HEAP through Community Concepts, 207-743-7716. Applying also screens you for free weatherization — insulation and air sealing that permanently cut the oil bill.
  • Prescriptions: Low Cost Drugs for the Elderly, and a free Part D plan review from SeniorsPlus every fall. Plans change yearly; the same drug can cost wildly different amounts. This review routinely saves people hundreds.
  • Medicare premiums and copays: the Medicare Savings Program, with no asset test. See Section 2.
  • Food: SNAP has a simplified application for seniors. Many older people assume they earn too much and never apply. Ask SeniorsPlus to screen you.
  • Phone/internet: ask your provider about low-income senior rates.

Before you act on anything here

Every dollar figure on this page is a 2026 amount, and nearly all of them change every year. Treat them as a guide for the conversation, not as the answer.

The one thing to be careful about: do not give away money or property, and do not put the house in a child’s name, before talking to a Maine elder law attorney. MaineCare looks back five years. A transfer that seems generous today can block coverage later, when the money is already gone and cannot be undone. Free advice is available at 1-800-750-5353 — use it before moving anything, not after.
